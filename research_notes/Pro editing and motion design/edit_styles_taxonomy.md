# Edit Styles Taxonomy (anime/manga/fan-edit and short-form: TikTok, YouTube, Instagram; AE, Alight Motion, CapCut)

Note on sourcing: the open web has very little authoritative writing on edit-style definitions. Most search hits were SEO spam, Fiverr gigs, or CapCut/Pippit template pages. The community's knowledge lives in TikTok/YouTube tutorials and Discords, which search tools can't index well. Every claim with a URL is cited. The per-style profiles below the cited findings are a **practitioner synthesis**, marked as inference. They reflect widely shared community conventions, but I couldn't verify each detail against a written source.

## Q1. What makes each style recognizable, and what separates a good one from a generic one?

### Takeaway
Only a few style labels have written definitions: candy, raw, flow, effects style, MMV/FMV, velocity, twixtor and AMV history. Most other labels (hype, sad, 3D/camera, overlay, chaotic) are hashtag-level descriptors defined by practice. Across all styles, the good-vs-generic gap comes down to beat-accurate sync, eased motion (curves rather than linear moves), restraint with presets, and clip choice that "flows".

### Cited Findings
- **Candy:** bright colors, lighthearted music, often borders and thick outlines on objects — [Viddertips (Tumblr)](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and). A Fandom wiki summary in search results described it as bright colors, bouncy motion, typography and geometric shapes, usually with pop songs, made in Vegas/AE/CapCut — [Fan Video Wiki: Candy editing](https://fanvideo.fandom.com/wiki/Candy_editing). The full page returned HTTP 402, so this rests on the search snippet only.
- **Candy CC:** candy-style color is often built with an S/Z-shaped curves graph — search summary of AM CC tutorials, [sccreationss AM CC tutorial](https://sccreationss.com/cc-editing-in-alight-motion-tutorial/).
- **Raw:** minimal editing (trims, crops, fades, basic grading). It focuses on story, emotion and theme rather than effects, and is sometimes seen as the opposite of effects style — [Viddertips](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and).
- **Flow:** more a quality than a style. It describes how well clips connect through similar color mood, movement and theme, and it is intuitive rather than technical — [Viddertips](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and). In TikTok usage, "flow edits" are tied to music and aesthetic visuals with transitions — [TikTok discover: flow edits](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/flow-edits).
- **Effects style:** visual effects and techniques take priority over story, sometimes motion graphics with no footage at all. **Motion graphics** means custom-made elements. **FMV** means fanart is the main material. **MMV** means manga is the main material — [Viddertips](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and).
- **Velocity:** speed changes timed to beats. You split at strong beats, keyframe speed, and ease it in the Graph Editor ("smoother curves avoid choppy changes"). Motion blur is matched to speed but overuse "can reduce video clarity". Bounce/rewind is done by duplicating and reversing. Export at 1080p60 — [AlightMotionLab](https://alightmotionlab.pro/how-to-do-velocity-edits-in-alight-motion/). Velocity is often paired with shake presets — [Pikoraa](https://pikoraa.com/trending-velocity-shake-editing-in-alight-motion-for-instagram-edit11/).
- **Shakes:** controlled by frequency, angle and magnitude. Phase is adjusted for smoothness and sync — [YouTube AM oscillate shake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ym5vhflNgzc). One guide suggests small position moves, subtle rotation and 0.15–0.30 s hit shakes, and warns that too much motion blur softens faces and text — [sccreationss smooth shake](https://sccreationss.com/smooth-shake-effect-in-alight-motion/).
- **CC:** built in layers: exposure/contrast, then tonal grade, then glow/atmosphere. Dropping a preset straight on causes blown highlights and crushed blacks — [sccreationss CC tutorial](https://sccreationss.com/cc-editing-in-alight-motion-tutorial/).
- **Twixtor/smooth:** anime is drawn at roughly 12–24 fps with held frames, and Twixtor interpolates in-betweens for smooth slow motion. The standard shape is a ramp: full speed in, slow at the peak, full speed out. Motion in many directions at once confuses the estimator. CapCut's interpolation lacks Twixtor's advanced controls — [animeworldtwixtor guide](https://animeworldtwixtor.com/how-to-use-twixtor-on-anime-clips-beginners-guide-2026/), [twixtorclips CapCut](https://twixtorclips.com/how-to-make-smooth-twixtor-clips-in-capcut/). A long-running AMV.org thread describes the speed-up-then-slow-down two-comp method and warns that moving hair is hard to interpolate — [AMV.org forum](https://www.animemusicvideos.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=120400). "Precut" twixtor clip packs are a common shared resource — [Ringwitdatwixtor](https://ringwitdatwixtor.com/anime-mix-twixtor-clips-for-editing/).
- **Classic AMV:** the first is credited to Jim Kaposztas (1982, two VCRs). Nu-metal fight AMVs (Linkin Park with DBZ/Naruto) became a meme. Named sub-styles include AMV Hell (comedy lyric/voice matching), lip-sync, hyper-pop (oversaturated, 60 fps), continuing match cuts, frame-by-frame cutouts, and hybrids. YouTube AMVs are longer, slower and use full scenes. IG/TikTok edits are shorter, faster and cut more often — [Sabukaru: Visual remix culture](https://sabukaru.online/articles/visual-remix-culture-the-legacy-of-amv).
- AnimeMusicVideos.org (est. 2000) used genre categories (action, comedy, drama, romance, parody, sentimental, trailer) to compare works — [Fanlore](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Amvs), [Henry Jenkins interview](https://www.henryjenkins.org/blog/2010/12/diy_media_2010_anime_music_vid_2.html).
- **Manga edit / manga animation:** typical AE workflow is to scan at 300+ DPI, import PSD/PNG, enable 3D layers, add and keyframe a camera, and optionally add glow on bubbles and particles — [Live Anime](https://www.liveanime.org/post/how-to-animate-manga-panels-after-effects). Tutorials cover 3D camera moves across panels and animating hair/body while keeping screentone texture — [YouTube 3D manga camera](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UlJ_elNM0a0), [Manga animation tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DMaRUDUCQtw), [Ultimate manga animation guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i1lgRsjK89Y). One-node cameras suit push-ins and trucks. Two-node cameras orbit a point of interest — [Adobe camera animation](https://helpx.adobe.com/th_th/after-effects/how-to/camera-animation.html). Commissioned "manga animations for your edits" are a Fiverr service category — [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/corezzola/create-manga-animations-for-your-edits).
- **Phonk:** drift phonk is credited to Russian producers and was mainstreamed by TikTok. By the mid-2020s the Brazilian offshoot (funk automotivo) became widely synonymous with the genre — [Wikipedia: Phonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonk). Its virality is tied to drift-car, gym and short-form edits — [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-phonk-music). Brazilian phonk is cited at 140–170 BPM, with high-impact drops — [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/drift-phonk). Purists dispute that Brazilian phonk is phonk at all — [Aesthetics Wiki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Brazilian_Phonk). Dvrst's "Close Eyes" soundtracked a 2021 TikTok glow-up format — [Wikipedia: Close Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Eyes). "Super slowed" Brazilian phonk remixes are common under anime edits — [TikTok discover](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/phonk-up-brazil).

### Inferences — per-style profiles (practitioner synthesis; unsourced unless a cited finding above supports them)
Format: **Defines / Pacing / Cuts / FX & transitions / Text / Color / Length & aspect / Music / Search terms / Mistakes**

- **AMV (classic):** a story or theme carried across a full song. Pacing follows the song. Cuts are on phrase and lyric, with impacts on hits. Effects are light (crossfades, flashes, a few masks). Text is a title card only. Grade is unified across sources. 3–5 min at 16:9. Rock/metal/EDM/indie. Search "AMV", "AMV contest", "AMV.org". *Mistakes:* clip-dump with no narrative, mismatched source quality, too many effects.
- **Anime edit (short-form):** 10–30 s built around one character or moment. Drop-centric: a calm build, then a hard drop. Cut density roughly every beat or half-beat after the drop. Effects are shakes, flashes, velocity, zoom punches and RGB split. Text is minimal (name or caption). Grade is punchy with contrast and glow. 9:16 at 1080×1920, 30/60 fps. Phonk, slowed songs, funk. Search "<character> edit", "#animeedit", "4k edit". *Mistakes:* preset shakes on every beat, a low-res source, a watermark covering the action.
- **Manga edit:** still B/W panels made kinetic. Pacing ranges from slow-burn to a fast drop. Cuts are panel-to-panel on beats. Effects are 3D camera pushes and parallax, panel splits, screentone/halftone, inverts on hits, ink-bleed and paper textures, speedlines, impact-frame flashes, and red/one-accent color. Text is Japanese SFX kept or enlarged, and speech bubbles revealed in sync. Grade is B/W with optional selective color. 9:16, 15–40 s. Phonk, slowed, ambient. Search "manga edit", "MMV", "manga panel edit". *Mistakes:* flat 2D zooms only, moiré or blurry scans, ignoring panel reading order.
- **Manga animation (animated panels):** the panels are rigged and moved: hair and cloth puppeting, eye blinks, rotoscoped separation, wind. Pacing is slower and showcases the craft. Cited AE workflow above. *Mistakes:* rubbery mesh warps, broken line weight, no secondary motion.
- **Mixtape edit:** a multi-song or multi-segment compilation. Each section is its own mini edit, with transitions or "tape" interludes between segments. Usually 1–3 min. Unclear online: definitions found were tutorial titles only.
- **Flow edit:** prioritizes continuity. Match cuts on motion direction and color, smooth transitions (zoom, spin, motion-blur whip), gentle easing and few hard shakes. Melodic music at mid tempo. Cited definition above. *Mistakes:* jarring color jumps, motion direction reversing between cuts.
- **Velocity edit:** speed ramps on beats (fast→slow→fast) with motion blur, often with shakes. Fast BPM (phonk/funk). Cited above. *Mistakes:* linear speed keys, ramps off-beat, blur turning faces to mush.
- **Twixtor/smooth edit:** buttery slow motion from interpolated frames with long, held, dramatic beats. Clean grade, little text. Cited above. *Mistakes:* warping artifacts on hair and limbs, interpolating held frames without first removing duplicates, slowing everything.
- **Aesthetic/soft edit:** a mood piece. Slow pacing, soft fades, light leaks, grain, bloom, pastel or film grade, small lowercase serif or handwritten text. Lo-fi, indie, slowed songs. Not clearly defined online: the term appears only as a hashtag. *Mistakes:* over-blurred, washed-out blacks.
- **Sad/emotional edit:** narrative beats, slower cuts, lyric text, desaturated or cool grade, held faces, and silence or a sudden cut before the climax. Slowed/reverb songs and piano. *Mistakes:* melodrama with no setup, effects fighting the emotion.
- **Hype/badass edit:** power moments. Hard cuts on every hit, heavy shakes, impact frames, flashes, chromatic aberration, high-contrast grade and big bold text. Phonk, trap, metal. *Mistakes:* constant intensity with no contrast, so the drop doesn't land.
- **Glitch edit:** datamosh, RGB split, slice displacement, digital noise and frame stutter on transients, plus glitched text. Electronic or hyperpop. *Mistakes:* glitch with no rhythm, glitching that hides the subject.
- **3D / camera edit:** footage or panels placed in 3D space, with camera orbits, Z-depth parallax, 3D flips/cubes and depth of field. AE 3D layers or AM 3D/camera features. *Mistakes:* unmotivated spinning, cardboard parallax with no depth separation.
- **Typography/lyric edit:** text is the subject. Kinetic type on the syllable, masks, tracking animation and type-on effects, often mixed with footage. *Mistakes:* unreadable timing (under ~0.3 s per word), too many fonts.
- **Candy edit:** bright saturated color, outlines and strokes, bouncy overshoot motion, shapes and stickers, pop music, often 2D-animated sources. Cited above.
- **Chaotic/meme edit:** ironic mixed media, sudden SFX, zoom-ins, deep-fry, out-of-context clips and meme captions. Examples: the "Where Is My" format and the ironic Bludsverse variants — [KYM](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/where-is-my-tiktok-edits). *Mistakes:* references that are already stale.
- **Raw edit:** cuts only and story-first. Cited above. In TikTok usage, "raw" can also mean unedited source clips (inference).
- **Transition-heavy edit:** every cut is a designed transition: zoom, spin, slide, warp, shape or light wipe. *Mistakes:* transitions that eat screen time from the content.
- **Overlay edit:** blend-mode layers on top of the footage: light leaks, particles, smoke, film burn, VHS, rain, and green-screen overlays. *Mistakes:* stacking overlays that muddy contrast.
- **Color/CC-focused edit:** the grade itself is the showcase, often split-screen before/after or a CC reveal. Cited CC layering above. *Mistakes:* pushing a preset without correcting first.
- **MMV:** a manga music video. Overlaps with manga edit; the term is older and longer-form — [Viddertips](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and).
- **GMV:** a game music video using gameplay or cinematic footage, same grammar as the AMV. CapCut templates show anime-style game edits such as MLBB — [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/explore/2026-anime-mobile-legends-game-trend-edit).
- **Phonk edit:** defined by the music (cowbell, distorted 808 at 140–170 BPM) more than by visuals. Cuts on the cowbell pattern, heavy velocity and shake, and often B/W manga. Cited above.
- **Comparison / "vs" edit:** split screens or alternating cuts between two characters, with stat text and "who wins" framing. Popular in power-scaling communities. Not well documented online.
- **Intros/outros/watermarks:** an editor's tag or logo animation (0.5–2 s), username watermarks to deter reposts, and credits for "CC: @ / clips: @". *Mistakes:* long intros that kill retention on short-form.
- **Additional styles found:** AMV Hell / comedy matching, lip-sync, hyper-pop (60 fps, oversaturated), match-cut "Animegraphy" compilations, frame-by-frame cutout memes ([Sabukaru](https://sabukaru.online/articles/visual-remix-culture-the-legacy-of-amv)), FMV/fanart videos ([Viddertips](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and)), and Viggle AI dance-swap edits ([meme.com](https://meme.com/memes/dance-if-youre-peak-can-i-join)).
- **Good vs generic, cross-style (inference):** good edits sync to the actual transient (snare, cowbell, 808) and use eased curves on speed, scale and position. They change energy deliberately (build → drop → breathe), match motion direction across cuts, use a consistent grade and clean upscaled sources, and add something original (rotoscope, custom type, 3D). Generic edits use a template or preset at default values, shake on every beat, have a mismatched source quality, and use the same zoom-punch throughout.

## Q2. Which techniques overlap between styles, and how do editors mix styles?

### Takeaway
Most "styles" are combinations of one shared toolkit (shake, velocity, flash, CC, transitions, text, overlays, 3D camera) with different weights. Editors routinely stack them, for example velocity plus shake, or phonk plus manga plus B/W.

### Cited Findings
- Velocity is commonly paired with shake presets — [Pikoraa](https://pikoraa.com/trending-velocity-shake-editing-in-alight-motion-for-instagram-edit11/).
- Sellers bundle shakes, CCs and transitions as separate interchangeable packs — [AM shake presets](https://rajantemplate.net/top-20-alight-motion-shake-effect-presets-free-download/).
- Twixtor is used inside speed ramps, the same move as velocity — [animeworldtwixtor](https://animeworldtwixtor.com/how-to-use-twixtor-on-anime-clips-beginners-guide-2026/).
- Freelance listings list velocity, shakes, flashes, interpolation, CC and text as the standard component menu of an anime edit — [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/xephroxz/make-professional-anime-edits-and-amvs), [Fiverr phonk manga](https://fiverr.com/jerggy_pelaez/create-anime-and-manga-phonk-edits).
- Viddertips frames flow as a quality that applies to any style, and raw vs effects as a spectrum — [Viddertips](https://www.tumblr.com/viddertips/86480880308/can-you-tell-me-the-different-editing-styles-and).

### Inferences
- Treat styles as presets over shared axes. **Energy:** sad/aesthetic → flow → hype/phonk. **FX density:** raw → effects/overlay/glitch. **Material:** anime, manga, game, fanart, live action. **Motion space:** 2D → 3D/camera. **Text weight:** none → typography.
- Common hybrids: phonk+manga+velocity, sad+typography, flow+twixtor, hype+glitch+3D, candy+typography, and a mixtape that chains several.
- A useful structure is a calm, soft, flow-style intro followed by a hype/velocity drop, which is "style switching" inside one edit.

## Q3. Current trends (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Short-form 9:16 edits dominate. Phonk, especially Brazilian funk/phonk and "ultra slowed" versions, remains the default soundtrack. CapCut templates drive mass trends, After Effects remains the high-end tool, and AI tools such as Viggle are entering meme edit formats.

### Cited Findings
- Brazilian phonk/funk automotivo became synonymous with phonk by the mid-2020s — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonk).
- "Where Is My" edit trend (June–July 2025): a slowed version of "Saravia Funk" with "who is this man" reveal captions. It started with Invincible and DBZ, then turned ironic — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/where-is-my-tiktok-edits).
- "Dance If You're Peak / Can I Join" (May 2025): characters swapped onto a dance clip via Viggle AI to Brazilian phonk ("DIA DELÍCIA"), spread through X reposts — [meme.com](https://meme.com/memes/dance-if-youre-peak-can-i-join).
- Edit screenshots became reaction images, e.g. "W Speed" (May 2025) — [KYM editorial](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/what-is-the-w-speed-meme-the-trend-of-ironic-ishowspeed-edits-screenshots-and-reaction-images-explained).
- CapCut template demand centers on JJK, Gojo and Sukuna, Blue Lock "Montagem" dance edits, and anime-styled game (MLBB) edits. Trend roundups name tweening and face-transformation formats — [CapCut 2026 MLBB](https://www.capcut.com/explore/2026-anime-mobile-legends-game-trend-edit), [genz.ai](https://genz.ai/trends/anime/drops/how-capcut-tweening-face-trends-shape-anime/), [TikTok CapCut tutorials](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/capcut-edit-tutorial-anime-2025). These are commercial/SEO sources and their dates are inconsistent.
- After Effects is still ranked the top anime editing tool, with CapCut positioned as template-driven — [worldmetrics](https://worldmetrics.org/best/anime-editing-software/) (affiliate-style source).
- Long AMVs are declining due to YouTube algorithms and Japanese rights-holder takedowns. Short IG/TikTok edits are rising — [Sabukaru](https://sabukaru.online/articles/visual-remix-culture-the-legacy-of-amv).

### Inferences
- For an editor app (AWIeffect), the highest-value presets are beat-synced velocity ramps with eased curves, controlled shakes (frequency/magnitude/phase), layered CC, 3D manga camera with parallax, impact flash/invert, and slowed-audio support.
- Ironic and meme formats cycle every few weeks, while the underlying hype/phonk/manga grammar stays stable.

- No reliable data on Reddit or Discord sentiment, platform-level stats, or 2026-specific trends beyond template listings. TikTok Creative Center was not accessed.
